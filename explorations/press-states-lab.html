Press States Lab

Three directions for touch feedback on the Redesign. Every phone is tappable: press, hold, slide off to cancel, scroll. Dials on the right change the active option live.

What each option changes

Orange values are new tokens. inMs and outMs replace today's single ms, so the touch-down can snap while the release eases.

How the three differ

  • A · Quiet: a small shrink and a small color step. Closest to today, minus the lag and the white flash.
  • B · Balanced: the brief's numbers. A clearly visible shrink and color step that stays calm.
  • C · Ringed: Balanced plus a thin light ring, so the press shows around your fingertip even when the finger hides the fill. Try both ring positions.

Shared by all three

  • The color changes the instant the board sees your finger; only the shrink animates in.
  • Release is slower than press (Emil: "the system's response should snap").
  • A quick tap holds its press for a minimum time, counted from touch-down, before the screen changes. A normal tap waits 0 ms extra. The caption under each phone shows what your last tap did.
  • Controls with no background (Back, Power, "+ Add", "All 5") fill their own shape on press, with no white flash.
  • The selected option in a list presses to dark orange, like every other orange control.

About the frame-rate dial

On the boards, the shrink is the expensive part to draw: every frame redraws the whole button. "10 fps" is a worst-case guess until we measure on the hardware. At 10 fps a 140 ms release is only one or two frames, which is why the color doesn't wait for the animation. Input is also only read once per frame, so a low frame rate delays the press too, just as it does on the board.

Slow-mo plays everything 5× slower so you can see each step.
